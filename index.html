Cadence framework overview

Abstract

Stage lighting brings music to life, turning sound into an immersive visual experience. Yet designing responsive lighting cues demands hours of tedious manual work from lighting designers. Existing audio-to-video models capture human movement or overall visual mood, but overlook the art of dynamic lighting. To bridge this gap, we first introduce LuSyClip, a concert lighting video benchmark for training and evaluating music-responsive stage lighting generation. We then propose Cadence, a framework that generates concert video whose stage lighting follows the music. Cadence builds on a state-of-the-art video backbone and adds three trainable modules: (i) a music-relative aligner that contrasts the response to the music with the response to silence, deciding what should change; (ii) a temporal control that decides when the change acts; and (iii) a spatial control that decides where it may act. On 1,032 held-out clips, Cadence clearly outperforms unadapted video baselines, reducing distributional error by 14.4 % and improving music-lighting synchronisation by 31.8 %. Together, these contributions give lighting designers a responsive and controllable tool for stage lighting design.
